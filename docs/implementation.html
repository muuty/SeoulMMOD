A guide to the MPGCN, ODCRN, and ODMixer adaptations: the original modeling ideas, the changes needed for SeoulMMOD, and the reasons behind them.

SeoulMMOD forecasts six transport modes over 6, 12, or 24 hours. We retain each model’s OD-matrix representation and core computation, and adapt the surrounding input, output, and training code to this task. The changes below aim to support stable training, practical data loading, and comparable evaluation. Their individual effects on forecasting accuracy have not been isolated in ablations.

Shared setup

Keep the OD axes intact.

The runner reshapes a batch from [batch, time, OD pairs, modes] to [batch, time, origins, destinations, modes]. Each model still operates on a full OD matrix. This preserves the distinction between origins and destinations that motivates these architectures.

Scaling and nonnegative flows. All three use log1p followed by training-fitted standardization per mode. The logarithm compresses the large range of flow volumes, and standardization centers the resulting inputs. Predictions are inverse-transformed for the MAE loss and evaluation. Negative predictions are clipped to zero during validation and testing, after returning to flow units. In particular, negative values in standardized space remain valid predictions.

A shared experiment pipeline. The adapters use BasicTS-compatible runners, chronological 70/10/20 splits, validation-MAE checkpoint selection, and at most 100 epochs with patience 10 in the released OD configs. Memory-mapped data and chunked preprocessing avoid loading the full subdistrict tensor into RAM. These choices do not remove the models’ internal memory costs: MPGCN and ODCRN still exceed the GPU budget at subdistrict scale.

01 / MPGCN

Retain the graph perspectives; expand the prediction head.

What stays. Each branch applies an LSTM to an OD pair’s history, followed by bidimensional graph convolutions over origins and destinations. One branch uses a static graph and the other uses time-dependent origin and destination graphs; their predictions are averaged. Our reference is the public PyTorch replication by underdoc-wang.

What changes, and why. The reference implementation produces one step with an output ReLU. Our head emits H × 6 values per OD pair, then reshapes them into hours and modes. This fits the benchmark’s multi-horizon task in one forward pass. We remove the head’s ReLU because standardized targets can be negative; enforcing nonnegativity there would prevent the model from representing low flows. Nonnegativity is applied later in the original flow units.

Graphs for hourly, multi-mode data. The released configs use a uniform static graph and 24 hour-of-day dynamic graphs computed from training-only flows summed over modes. Origins are compared through their destination profiles (rows), and destinations through their origin profiles (columns). The configs use cosine similarity, so similar profiles receive larger affinity. This differs from the reference loader’s seven-step period, supplied static adjacency, and cosine-distance calculation; its destination calculation compares a column with a row. The 24-hour period aligns the graphs with daily mobility cycles, while the uniform branch supplies citywide connectivity. These are explicit graph adaptations, and their accuracy effects have not been isolated.

Implementation efficiency. Normalized matrix products replace Python loops over zone pairs when building the cosine graphs, with a small denominator offset for empty profiles. The averages are accumulated in chunks, and the 24 graphs are constructed once at initialization. This keeps the graph-based core while making preprocessing practical for hourly OD data; no measured speedup or accuracy gain is claimed here.

02 / ODCRN

Keep the recurrent OD dynamics.

What stays. We retain the OD-convolutional recurrent cells, learned dynamic origin and destination graphs, and encoder–decoder architecture. The decoder still generates future matrices autoregressively, feeding each prediction into the next step. Graph construction and recurrent updates follow the reference implementation.

What changes, and why. The input and output channel count is set to six, and the prediction horizon follows the benchmark configuration. The adapter supplies Seoul’s static adjacency and connects the model to the shared matrix-shaped data, log-standardization, MAE objective, and checkpoint selection. This lets the original recurrent mechanism handle multi-mode hourly observations under the same evaluation protocol as the other baselines. It does not replace the recurrent decoder with a direct prediction head or simplify away the dynamic graphs.

03 / ODMixer

Extend the projections, preserve the mixers.

What stays. The Channel Mixer, origin- and destination-wise Multi-view Mixer, residual connections, normalization, and Bidirectional Trend Learner are retained. The current-window and previous-day branches share the encoder and interact through the trend learner. Both branches contribute an MAE term during training.

What changes, and why. The reference model embeds a single-mode time sequence for each OD pair and outputs one step. We combine the T input hours and six modes into a T × 6 feature vector for the input projection. The output projection produces H × 6 values, which are reshaped back to hours and modes. The origin and destination axes remain intact throughout. This gives the existing mixers access to all mode histories without adding a new spatial or mode-interaction module.

Keep the daily trend input. The second branch receives the corresponding window shifted back by 24 hours, and retains its auxiliary prediction loss. Its future target is used for training supervision, not as an inference input. The adaptation therefore preserves the original reason for having two branches: learning from the relationship between the recent window and a recurring temporal pattern.
