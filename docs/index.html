01 / The dataset

See how the whole city moves.

Single-service data capture only part of urban travel. SeoulMMOD brings six modes into a common hourly origin–destination representation, with estimated trip volumes across the same geographic zones.

Modes may provide alternatives for a journey or connect through transfers. Their aligned histories make it possible to study whether multi-mode information improves forecasting.

Flows are population-scaled estimates derived from mobile-phone data. Aggregate mode shares do not identify individual substitutions or transfers. The benchmark covers Seoul; cross-city generalization is not established.

Finding 02

Multi-mode inputs reduce MAE by 0.48% for STID and 3.52% for STAEformer. Predicting all modes with one model raises MAE by 21.7% and 9.6% relative to all-six-to-one.

Single-run comparisons. Predicting all modes with one model also changes total capacity, so the results do not isolate output sharing as the cause of the increase.

04 / OD sparsity

Overall error hides different failures.

Group each OD pair and mode by how often its training flow is positive. Inspect all targets together, or separate positive and zero targets.

Finding 03

HA has the lowest reported MAE on rare flows, iTransformer on sparse and medium flows, and STAEformer on dense flows. On rare flows, zero-target errors vary far more across models than positive-target errors.

06 / Implementation details

The same core ideas, adapted to SeoulMMOD.

Our OD baselines retain their origin–destination structure. The adapters extend their inputs and outputs to six modes and multiple forecast hours, with explicit choices for graph construction and scaling.

MPGCNLSTM and two graph-convolution branches; a multi-horizon head and hourly flow graphs.↗ ODCRNDynamic OD graphs and recurrent decoding; six-mode channels and a common training pipeline.↗ ODMixerChannel and Multi-view Mixers plus bidirectional trend learning; expanded time–mode projections.↗

Read what is preserved, what changes, and why, with links to the reference implementations and the released code. These adaptations support the benchmark setting; we do not attribute accuracy gains to individual changes without an ablation.
